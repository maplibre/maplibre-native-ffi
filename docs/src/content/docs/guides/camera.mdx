This guide shows how to read and change a map's camera, from your code and from
gestures. A [](api:mln_camera) has a center, a zoom, a bearing, and a pitch.

## Read the camera

Read the camera from the map's snapshot with [](api:mln_map_get_snapshot). To
read it after your pending changes apply, use [](api:mln_map_get_camera)
instead.

<Snippet page="camera" region="read" />

## Move the camera

To move the camera, send a camera update with [](api:mln_map_update_camera) in
jump mode. Set only the fields you want to change.

<Snippet page="camera" region="jump" />

## Animate the camera

To animate, send the update in ease or fly mode with a duration. Ease moves
straight to the target, while fly zooms out, travels, and zooms back in.

To act when an animation ends, set a [](api:mln_camera_transition_handler).

To stop an animation where it is, cancel the map's camera transitions with
[](api:mln_map_cancel_camera_transitions).

<Snippet page="camera" region="animate" />

## Fit the camera

To show an area, fit the camera to its bounding box with
[](api:mln_map_fit_camera_to_lat_lng_bounds), at once or with an animation. For
a list of coordinates or a GeoJSON geometry, use
[](api:mln_map_fit_camera_to_lat_lngs) or [](api:mln_map_fit_camera_to_geometry)
instead.

To get the camera without moving to it, use
[](api:mln_map_camera_for_lat_lng_bounds), [](api:mln_map_camera_for_lat_lngs),
or [](api:mln_map_camera_for_geometry).

<Snippet page="camera" region="fit" />

## Limit the camera

To keep the camera within an area, set the map's bounds with
[](api:mln_map_set_bounds). Bounds limit the center to an area, and the zoom and
pitch to ranges.

<Snippet page="camera" region="limit" />

## Apply gestures

A [](api:mln_gesture_recognizer) turns your app's input into camera movement.
Create one for the map with [](api:mln_gesture_recognizer_create), and pass it
each input event with [](api:mln_gesture_recognizer_handle): pointers going
down, moving, and up, wheel and key movement as deltas, and pinches that the
platform recognizes. Screen points are in logical pixels from the map's top-left
corner, and every input and tick takes a time from the same clock.

The recognizer drags, pinches, twists, and tilts the map, and keeps it gliding
after a flick. Each result says when it next needs
[](api:mln_gesture_recognizer_tick), and reports taps, long presses, and notches
for your app to act on. Its options set how each part of the camera glides and
snaps, so it can match how your platform scrolls.

<Snippet page="camera" region="gesture" />

On a globe, drags and zooms keep the bearing by default. If your users can
rotate the map, turn on [](api:mln_map_viewport_options.globe_free_bearing) with
[](api:mln_map_set_viewport_options) so they turn the bearing with the planet
instead, and a drag can cross a pole.

## Convert coordinates

A [](api:mln_map_projection) converts between screen points and coordinates for
one view of the map. Create it for the map's current view, or for the view of a
frame result, and create a new one after the camera moves. Over terrain, it
converts on the ground that the map drew, and
[](api:mln_map_projection_get_terrain_elevation) reads the ground's height.
Release it when you're done. On a globe, a coordinate on the far side still has
a screen point, so check [](api:mln_map_projection_is_location_occluded) before
you draw at it.

<Snippet page="camera" region="convert" />
