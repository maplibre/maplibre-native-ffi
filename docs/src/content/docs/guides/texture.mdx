This guide shows how to render a continuous map into textures for another
renderer to draw, such as a UI framework, a game engine, or a compositor. It
builds on the frame loop from
[Render to a window](/maplibre-native-ffi/guides/window/#render-frames).

To read a map's pixels on the CPU instead, render offscreen, as in
[Render images](/maplibre-native-ffi/guides/images/).

## Attach a texture ring

The session renders into a ring of textures that you create on your GPU device,
so your renderer can draw one frame while the session renders the next. Create
two textures at the map's size in device pixels, which is its logical size times
its scale factor.

<Aside type="note">
  The snippets below use Vulkan. For another backend, use that backend's calls
  to attach and replace the texture ring, and to get each frame's texture. The
  other steps are the same.
</Aside>

On Vulkan, if your renderer submits to the same queue as the session, pass a
lock in the attach options, and hold it around your own submissions.

As with a window, use your graphics thread as the driver for OpenGL and WebGPU
textures, and the library thread otherwise. See
[Service driver work](/maplibre-native-ffi/guides/window/#service-driver-work).

<Snippet page="texture" region="attach-options" />

Then attach your textures, with your graphics context, such as your device and
queue, and the textures' size in device pixels. Keep the textures alive until
you replace them, or until the session detaches.

<Snippet page="texture" region="attach-borrowed" />

## Draw each frame

Run the window's frame loop, but without [](api:MLN_FRAME_DEMAND_PRESENT), since
your renderer shows the frames instead.

When a frame renders, acquire it with [](api:mln_render_session_acquire_frame)
and get its texture for your backend. The frame's slot is the index of its
texture in your ring.

When your reads finish, release the frame with
[](api:mln_acquired_frame_release), so the session can render into that texture
again. If your reads may still run on the GPU, pass a sync point of a kind that
[](api:mln_render_session_capabilities) lists in its consumer sync kinds, and
the session waits for it on the GPU. Keep at most one frame acquired, or the
next frame waits for a free texture, and release every acquired frame before you
close the session.

<Snippet page="texture" region="acquire" />

## Resize the textures

The session renders at the size of your textures, so to resize, give it new
ones. First, release every acquired frame. Then create textures at the new size,
and set them as the session's target, with as many textures as before. Once the
replacement completes, destroy the old textures.
