This guide presents a continuous map to a window or view that you create, and
keeps it updated as the map changes. It starts from a runtime and a map in
continuous mode. To create them, see
[Create a map](/maplibre-native-ffi/guides/create-a-map/).

## Attach a surface

Create the window with your platform or windowing library. Then get its surface
handle for your backend:

- Metal: a
  [`CAMetalLayer`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cametallayer)
- Vulkan: a
  [`VkSurfaceKHR`](https://registry.khronos.org/vulkan/specs/latest/man/html/VkSurfaceKHR.html)
- OpenGL: an
  [EGL window surface](https://registry.khronos.org/EGL/sdk/docs/man/html/eglCreateWindowSurface.xhtml),
  or an `HDC` with WGL
- WebGPU: a
  [canvas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUCanvasContext)

The snippets use Vulkan and start from that handle.

Attach the surface to the map at the window's size and scale factor. Pass a
frame wake. The next section uses it. Wait for the attach to finish.

Use the library thread as the driver, unless your backend needs your graphics
thread. See
[Choose a render target](/maplibre-native-ffi/guides/rendering/choose-a-target/#pick-a-driver).

<Snippet page="window" region="attach" />

## Render frames

Ask for one frame at a time, starting after the attach finishes. Ask the frame
to present, and to wait for the map's next update. An idle map then renders
nothing.

The frame wake can run on a library thread. Have it schedule a drain of the
frame results on your event loop.

<Snippet page="window" region="frame-wake" />

When the result arrives, ask for the next frame at the next display refresh.
This renders at most once per refresh, and shows every change made in between.

When a frame fails, close the session and attach a new one.

<Snippet page="window" region="frame-loop" />

## Resize the window

When the window's size changes, resize the session to the new width and height.
The session resizes the map too. Keep asking for frames. Frames render at the
new size once the map has applied it.

To change the scale factor, or if the platform gives you a new surface for the
new size, set the surface as the session's target instead. See the next section.

<Snippet page="window" region="resize" />

## Handle a lost surface

When the platform takes the surface away, such as when your application goes to
the background, stop asking for frames. Release the session's target, and wait
for the release before the surface is destroyed. The session keeps its tiles and
other renderer state.

When the platform gives you a surface again, set it as the session's target.
Wait for that to finish, then ask for a frame.

<Snippet page="window" region="lost-surface" />

## Render on your graphics thread

With the graphics-thread driver, the session's graphics work runs only when you
service the session on your graphics thread. Make the target's context current
there first.

Pass a driver work wake when you attach. Each time it fires, schedule a service
of the session on your graphics thread, even while you ask for no frames.
Attaching, detaching, and releasing the target finish only through service.

<Snippet page="window" region="service" />

## Close the session

Stop asking for frames. Close the session, and wait for it to finish. With the
graphics-thread driver, detach the session first, and keep servicing it until
the detach finishes.

Destroy the surface after the session closes. Then close the map and the
runtime, as in [Create a map](/maplibre-native-ffi/guides/create-a-map/#close).

<Snippet page="window" region="close" />
